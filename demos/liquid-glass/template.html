How the bend is made

For every shape, a canvas draws a displacement map from the rounded rectangle's signed distance field. Inside the bezel, each pixel points toward the centre, with a strength that follows the slope of a convex squircle profile. feDisplacementMap then samples the room along those vectors, so edges magnify and pull in light the way a thick pane does.

How it moves

Everything runs on damped springs, not timed easing. Pieces condense into place on load. Dragging stretches the glass along its velocity and squashes it across, then it wobbles back when you let go. Pressing thickens the pane, so the rim bends harder. The tab droplet's leading edge moves on a stiffer spring than its trailing edge, which is what makes it look poured.

Why it looks wet

Red, green and blue are displaced by slightly different amounts and recombined, which gives the thin colour fringe at the rim. An inset highlight lit from the top-left, a screen-blended sheen and a two-layer drop shadow finish the material. The flat centre stays sharp, which is the difference from frosted glass.
